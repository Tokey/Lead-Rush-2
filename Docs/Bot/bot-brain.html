Chapter 1 · Perception

Seeing you

The bot looks every 0.1 s. You are visible when you are inside its vision cone, within range and not behind a wall. Visibility fills a detection meter; at 1.0 the bot has spotted you. Closer, moving and recently-firing targets fill it faster, crouching slows it, and within 6 m anything within ±100° of its gaze counts (a 200° total acceptance sector), because you notice someone right next to you. Drag the orange dot to move.

Fill rate per second = detection speed × (0.9 + 9 / distance), × 1.35 if you move faster than 2 m/s, × 2.2 for 1 s after a loud shot, × 1.4 within 12° of its gaze, × 0.7 if crouched. Out of sight it drains at 0.5/s, and the bot forgets you after 12 s. Its first shot also waits a reaction time that grows from 0.76× (you were already in view) to 1.58× (you appeared far to the side). BotPerception.cs

Chapter 2 · Decision

Choosing what to do

About four times a second the brain walks down a fixed priority list and takes the first goal whose conditions hold. Set up a situation on the right and watch which goal wins, and why the ones above it were skipped.

    Chances such as "take cover 60%" and "flank" use the difficulty's dice; the list shows when a goal is allowed to roll.
    Chapter 3 · Motor control

    Aiming like a hand

    Bots never snap. A big turn onto you is a quick ballistic flick whose duration follows Fitts's law (far and small targets take longer), shaped like a human arm movement (the minimum-jerk curve: slow, fast, slow), landing with some scatter. Then a feedback loop corrects onto the target, with a little tremor that grows with fast movements. Faint lines are other attempts with different scatter.

    Chapter 4 · Recoil

    Fighting recoil

    The bot's gun climbs exactly like yours. It only starts pulling down after a human reaction delay (65-135 ms), and the longer it sprays the worse its pull-down gets ("spray heat"). That leaves a window where its shots go high: your counter-play.

    AK-12 climb ≈ 1.05° per shot from the hip (× 0.55 in ADS) at 700 rounds/min. Pull-down strength = clamp(aim speed / 10.5, 0.55-0.92) × (1 − 0.22 × spray heat); heat rises 0.19 per shot from the hip, 0.13 in ADS, and cools at 0.95/s.

    Chapter 5 · Adaptive difficulty

    Learning your style

    On Adaptive, bots read how you play. Win duels and they sharpen; lose a few and they ease off, so fights stay close. Your accuracy, headshots and whether you rush or camp also shape how they fight. Play a few duels with the buttons.

    Skill moves +0.045 per win (+0.03 on a streak, +0.02 for a headshot, + a little for accuracy) and −0.040 per loss (−0.035 more on a losing streak), always between 0.12 and 0.96. AdaptiveDifficultyTracker.cs

    Chapter 6 · Next

    Plugging in an LLM

    The plan is LLM-driven bots for team deathmatch. The language model never steers the crosshair: it reads a compact state summary every 1.5 s and gives orders, while the human-limited aim and movement above stay in charge of the hands. A full replacement brain is also possible.

    How it gives orders

    class LlmCommander : MonoBehaviour, IBotDirectiveProvider {
  public bool TryGetDirective(BotController bot,
      BotBlackboard bb, out BotDirective d) {
    // ask the model, map the reply to an order
    d = new BotDirective { order = BotOrder.Flank,
                           duration = 8f };
    return true;
  }
}

    Orders: HoldPosition, MoveTo, Attack, Flank, Retreat, FollowLeader. Or implement IBotBrain to replace the whole decision layer.
