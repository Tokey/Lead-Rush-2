# B30 - Realistic glass materials

Use HDRP Lit transparent materials with thin refraction for window-like panes; Unity documents
glass IOR as `1.52`, and HDRP's thin refraction model is intended for surfaces such as windows.
Use a nominal `0.008 m` thickness for the material family, while keeping pane geometry thin
and letting the shader model the optical surface.
Clean glass keeps high smoothness and low alpha; dirty glass uses a procedural grime/streak
mask to lower smoothness and increase edge/dirt visibility without making the pane opaque.
Cracked glass uses a separate procedural crack normal map and lower smoothness so fractures
catch highlights; it remains transparent until the existing destructible-pane logic breaks it.
The builder creates `M_LR2_Glass_Clean`, `M_LR2_Glass_Dirty`, `M_LR2_Glass_Cracked`, plus
the reusable grime and crack PNGs under `Assets/LR2/Materials/Environment/Masks`.
Materials are generated by `LR2 > Build Glass Materials`; map builders should reference these
assets rather than embedding private textures or commercial material packs.

Sources: [Unity HDRP refractive material guide](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.7/manual/create-a-refractive-material.html),
[Unity HDRP refraction guide](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@14.0/manual/refraction-use.html).
