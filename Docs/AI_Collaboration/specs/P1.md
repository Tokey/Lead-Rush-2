# P1 - Indoor and outdoor weapon sound tails

LR2 already distinguishes roofed positions with `LR2Audio.IsIndoors()` and
`WeaponAudioProfile.TailFor()`, and it plays early wall reflections from
`LR2Firearm.PlayEchoes()`. The remaining work is to make the indoor/outdoor
transition consistent, audible without masking gun punch, and measurable.

## Targets

1. Keep the close fire/mechanical layers stable; swap only the environmental
   tail based on the shooter position.
2. Use a dedicated indoor tail for covered interiors and warehouses, with
   roughly 250-500 ms early decay and a longer but lower late tail.
3. Use a dry outdoor tail with short close reflections and little low-frequency
   buildup. Strong courtyard or canyon reflections may be 250-700 ms.
4. At equal calibrated loudness, a listener in 3D should hear the indoor tail
   around 3-6 dB more than the outdoor tail, not more than 1 dB of close
   muzzle punch difference.
5. Do not repeat an identical reverb tail on rapid fire, and do not let tails
   overlap into a continuous brick wall of sound. Limit layered tails to 3-4
   overlapping voices.
6. Preserve the existing distance bands and travel-time delay. Reverb positions
   must remain occlusion-aware.

## Current LR2 implementation

- `LR2Audio.IsIndoors()` classifies a position using two upward environment rays.
- `WeaponAudioProfile.TailFor(bool)` selects `fireTailInterior` or `fireTail`.
- `LR2Firearm.PlayEchoes()` probes eight directions, rejects terrain hits and
  reflections under 5 m, delays wall copies by round-trip speed of sound, and
  reduces wall copy gain indoors.
- `LR2MapBuilder` adds warehouse, room, workshop, yard, and city reverb zones.

## Acceptance

- Indoor and outdoor muzzle-tail comparison clips logged by the QA runner.
- A visible no-echo outdoor forest pass and a covered warehouse pass.
- No clipping or tail buildup in 600 RPM fire.
- Both assemblies compile.
- Unity-visible evidence if the Unity process is free.
